Reference Cases

Reproducible replenishment cases

Five synthetic scenarios exercise the replenishment method end to end. For each one, the expected values were derived by hand. They were then checked against the calculator engine and against an independent oracle that recomputes everything in exact fractions. A case is published only when all three agree.

Synthetic inputs. These are not observed household behavior, consumption statistics, or benchmarks.

Cases

  • HRR-001Stable consumption
  • HRR-002Variable consumption
  • HRR-003Short lead-time replenishment
  • HRR-004Delayed delivery and stockout exposure
  • HRR-005Fixed subscription interval versus replenishment window

How to check a case yourself

  1. Work through the hand derivation shown for the case. Every step uses only the formulas in the calculator methodology.
  2. Enter the same inputs in the calculator. Its JSON record should match the results block of the case record.
  3. Download the manifest and a record, and compare the record's sha256 with the manifest. Records use the serialization the manifest describes.
  4. From a copy of the public repository, run node verification/build-reference-records.js --check. It recomputes every case with the engine and the oracle, and fails if any value or file differs.

Records follow an internal record schema (version 0.1). It is a HygieneRoll contract, not an external standard. Cases come from the registry version 0.1, checked against engine v0.2.1 and ontology 0.1.

Stable consumption (synthetic)

A household with steady use and no variability allowance. It shows the simplest path from supply to a single depletion day, a single planning trigger, and a reorder threshold.

Timeline

Results

  • Supply runs out day 28.
  • Order window day 21.
  • Reorder threshold 3.01 rolls (lead-time demand 1.29 + reserve 1.72).
  • Delivery 3 days late: buffer absorbs it, no days without supply.
  • Delivery 7 days late: stockout at both bounds, 3 days without supply.

Inputs

ParameterValueUnitTerm
categoryPAPER_HYGIENEcodeHRO-001

usageModeweeklycodeHRO-006
weeklyUsage3rolls per weekHRO-006
variabilityPercent0percentHRO-007
leadTimeDays3daysHRO-010
bufferDays4daysHRO-011

Assumptions and limitations

  • Use is exactly 3 rolls every week.
  • Lead time and buffer are whole days chosen for the example.
  • A 0% variability allowance gives a single day. It does not mean use is certain.

Hand derivation

  1. Weekly method: r = 3 rolls per week. Variability 0%, so slow = fast = 3.
  2. Depletion: 12 / 3 × 7 = 28 days exactly. Single day 28.
  3. Planning trigger: 28 − 3 − 4 = 21.
  4. Replenishment quantity blank, so R = 12 and cadence = 28 days.
  5. Lead-time demand: 3 × 3 / 7 = 1.2857… → rounded up to 1.29. Reserve: 4 × 3 / 7 = 1.7142… → 1.72. Threshold 1.29 + 1.72 = 3.01. 12 > 3.01, so not at or below.
  6. Slip +3: lead 6. Re-planned trigger 28 − 6 − 4 = 18. Ordered on day 21, arrival 27. 27 ≤ 28, so no gap. 27 > 28 − 4 = 24, so the buffer is drawn.
  7. Slip +7: lead 10. Re-planned trigger 28 − 10 − 4 = 14. Arrival 21 + 10 = 31. Gap 31 − 28 = 3 at both bounds.

Method and ontology terms
  • Consumption rate: weekly method: r = weekly_usage; duration method: r = current_quantity / duration_weeks
  • Usage bounds: slow = r × (1 − v); fast = r × (1 + v); v = variability_percent / 100

Variable consumption (synthetic)

Use is entered as how long supply usually lasts, with a 25% variability allowance and a larger replenishment quantity. It shows how the allowance widens every window and how floor and ceiling rounding set each end.

Timeline

Results

  • Supply runs out days 33–56.
  • Order window days 23–46.
  • Reorder threshold 0.9 bottles (lead-time demand 0.45 + reserve 0.45).
  • Delivery 3 days late: buffer absorbs it, no days without supply.
  • Delivery 7 days late: stockout possible at the fast bound, 0–2 days without supply.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Three bottles usually last six weeks.
  • Actual use falls anywhere between 25% slower and 25% faster than that rate.
  • The 25% allowance is a planning range chosen for the example. It is not a measured spread or a confidence interval.

Hand derivation

  1. Duration method: r = 3 / 6 = 0.5 bottles per week. v = 0.25, so slow = 0.5 × 0.75 = 0.375 and fast = 0.5 × 1.25 = 0.625.
  2. Depletion earliest: 3 / 0.625 × 7 = 33.6 → floor 33. Latest: 3 / 0.375 × 7 = 56 exactly → ceiling 56.
  3. Planning trigger: 33 − 5 − 5 = 23 and 56 − 5 − 5 = 46.
  4. Cadence with R = 4: 4 / 0.625 × 7 = 44.8 → floor 44. 4 / 0.375 × 7 = 74.67 → ceiling 75.
  5. Lead-time demand: 5 × 0.625 / 7 = 0.4464… → 0.45. Reserve: the same, 0.45. Threshold 0.90. 3 > 0.90.
  6. Slip +3: lead 8. Re-planned 33 − 13 = 20 and 56 − 13 = 43. Ordered on day 23, arrival 31 ≤ 33, so no gap. 31 > 33 − 5 = 28, so the buffer is drawn.
  7. Slip +7: lead 12. Re-planned 33 − 17 = 16 and 56 − 17 = 39. Arrival 35. At the fast bound 35 − 33 = 2 days without supply. At the slow bound 35 < 56, so none.

Delayed delivery and stockout exposure (synthetic)

Lead time plus buffer is longer than the supply lasts. The planning trigger is clamped to Now, supply is already at or below the reorder threshold, and any delay leaves days without supply at both bounds.

Timeline

Fixed subscription interval versus replenishment window (synthetic)

A fixed 35-day delivery schedule is compared with the item's replenishment cadence. The interval falls after the cadence window, so each delivery arrives after the previous quantity would run out.

Timeline

Results

  • Supply runs out days 24–33.
  • Order window days 17–26.
  • Reorder threshold 0.58 bottles (lead-time demand 0.33 + reserve 0.25).
  • Fixed 35-day interval: later than the cadence window, drift 2 to 11 days per delivery.
  • Delivery 3 days late: buffer absorbs it, no days without supply.
  • Delivery 7 days late: stockout possible at the fast bound, 0–4 days without supply.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The household holds or is considering a fixed delivery every 35 days.
  • Each delivery adds 2 bottles.
  • The comparison is arithmetic for one item. It is not a recommendation to change a subscription.

Hand derivation

  1. Weekly method: r = 0.5 bottles per week. v = 0.15, so slow = 0.425 and fast = 0.575.
  2. Depletion earliest: 2 / 0.575 × 7 = 24.35 → floor 24. Latest: 2 / 0.425 × 7 = 32.94 → ceiling 33.
  3. Planning trigger: 24 − 4 − 3 = 17 and 33 − 4 − 3 = 26.
  4. Cadence with R = 2 equals the depletion window, 24 to 33.
  5. Interval 35 > 33, so it is later than the window. Drift per delivery: 35 − 33 = 2 to 35 − 24 = 11 days. Supply shrinks each delivery.
  6. Lead-time demand: 4 × 0.575 / 7 = 0.3285… → 0.33. Reserve: 3 × 0.575 / 7 = 0.2464… → 0.25. Threshold 0.58.
  7. Slip +3: lead 7. Re-planned 24 − 10 = 14 and 33 − 10 = 23. Ordered on day 17, arrival 24 = 24, so no gap. 24 > 24 − 3 = 21, so the buffer is drawn.
  8. Slip +7: lead 11. Re-planned 24 − 14 = 10 and 33 − 14 = 19. Arrival 28. 28 − 24 = 4 days at the fast bound, none at the slow bound.

Method and ontology terms
  • Consumption rate: weekly method: r = weekly_usage; duration method: r = current_quantity / duration_weeks
  • Usage bounds: slow = r × (1 − v); fast = r × (1 + v); v = variability_percent / 100
  • Depletion interval: earliest = floor((q / fast) × 7); latest = ceil((q / slow) × 7); at v = 0 both equal round_half_up((q / r) × 7)
  • Lead-time demand: ceil_0.01(lead_time_days × fast / 7)
  • Reserve stock: ceil_0.01(safety_buffer_days × fast / 7)
  • Reorder threshold: lead_time_demand + reserve_stock
  • Planning trigger: max(0, depletion − lead_time_days − safety_buffer_days), each end separately
  • Replenishment cadence: same day-window rule as the depletion interval, with replenishment_quantity in place of q
  • Subscription compatibility: EARLIER_THAN_WINDOW if interval < cadence.earliest; LATER_THAN_WINDOW if interval > cadence.latest; otherwise WITHIN_WINDOW; drift = interval − cadence, from latest (min) to earliest (max)
  • Stockout exposure: arrival = order_day + lead_time; max = max(0, ceil(arrival − depletion.earliest)); min = max(0, ceil(arrival − depletion.latest))
  • Lead-time slip: for each offset s: trigger(depletion, lead_time + s, buffer); stockout_exposure(order_day = trigger.earliest, lead_time + s)
  • Next household return: items with minimum planning_trigger.earliest, in input order
