Household Hygiene Replenishment Reference

A governed vocabulary for depletion, planning, cadence, return, and recurring household hygiene.

Definitions, evidence, interpretation, and illustrative models are labeled separately.

How to read this reference

These entries are HygieneRoll working definitions. They say how this model uses a term. They are not empirical market facts, and they are not a claim that the wording is an industry standard, an official taxonomy, or a scientifically validated law.

Foundation v0.1 admits no external observations. A statement about markets, households, or companies would need an admitted source before it could appear here. An example used only to show the logic would be marked illustrative, and would not be described as measured behavior.

  • Project definitionA distinction HygieneRoll chooses and names. It points to the public page or method where the concept is used.
  • Observed evidenceA factual statement about the external world. It is published only with an admitted source. None are admitted in v0.1.
  • Derived interpretationA reading of admitted evidence. It stays in interpretive language and does not pretend the source stated the conclusion.
  • Illustrative modelA scenario or diagram used to explain logic. It is not a measured household or an average.

Core reference objects

HRK-001

HygieneRoll working definition

Replenishment vs Subscription

Definition

Within the HygieneRoll model, a subscription describes a recurring commerce arrangement, and replenishment describes planning around an item approaching renewed need or depletion.

Key distinction

A subscription can be one mechanism for recurring commerce. Replenishment logic is concerned with the timing of need. The two can coincide, and they can also come apart: a fixed shipping interval does not, by itself, describe when a particular item in a household is running low.

Why it matters inside the model

The model asks when the household is likely to need the item again, using the assumptions the visitor supplies. It does not treat a billing cycle as the definition of need.

Related artifact

Operationalized on the calculator and described in the replenishment model. Related entries: Planning Trigger and Illustrative Cadence.

Evidence posture

Project-defined. Defined within the HygieneRoll model. No external evidence is attached.

HRK-002

HygieneRoll working definition

Household Depletion Clock

Definition

The HygieneRoll model represents a household as containing multiple recurring essentials whose supply can deplete on different timelines, and it treats each item as an independent replenishment clock.

Key distinction

The household is one customer relationship. Its consumption is not one clock. Paper, soap, and cleaner can reach a planning threshold on different days. The model does not collapse those clocks into a single monthly schedule.

Why it matters inside the model

The calculator operationalizes this by estimating each item on its own and then identifying the soonest planning trigger. That household summary does not average unlike items. This is a representation chosen by the model, not a research finding about how many cycles households have.

Related artifact

Operationalized by calculateHousehold() in the calculator engine, and visible when more than one item is estimated on the calculator. Related entries: Planning Trigger and Re-entry.

Evidence posture

Project-defined. Defined within the HygieneRoll model. No external evidence is attached.

HRK-003

HygieneRoll working definition

Planning Trigger

Definition

In HygieneRoll calculator v0.1, the planning trigger is the rounded depletion timing minus lead time minus safety buffer, clamped at zero. It is a scenario-derived planning threshold.

Key distinction

The trigger is the day the planning window opens under the visitor’s assumptions. It is not an optimal reorder point, a scientific reorder date, or a recommended purchase date. A result at or below zero is shown as now, not as a negative day.

Why it matters inside the model

Depletion timing alone does not say when a replenishment would need to be underway. Lead time and buffer are subtracted so the threshold sits earlier than the estimated depletion. The rounding rule is the one specified in the calculator methodology.

Related artifact

Specified in CALCULATOR_METHODOLOGY.md and calculated on the calculator. Related entry: Lead Time and Safety Buffer.

Evidence posture

Project-defined. Defined within the HygieneRoll model. No external evidence is attached.

HRK-004

HygieneRoll working definition

Lead Time and Safety Buffer

Definition

Within HygieneRoll calculator v0.1, lead time is the time assumed between initiating replenishment and availability or arrival.

Within HygieneRoll calculator v0.1, the safety buffer is a user-selected additional time before estimated depletion. It is not a medically safe stock level, an industry optimum, or a statistical confidence interval.

Key distinction

Lead time is about arrival. The buffer is extra time the visitor wants before the estimated depletion. They are separate inputs. Either may be zero. Neither is supplied by a hidden default, and neither is a health or sanitation standard.

Why it matters inside the model

Both are subtracted from depletion timing to form the planning trigger. Changing either one changes the threshold without changing the usage rate. The model does not infer a correct buffer from household size or from a category average.

Related artifact

Entered on the calculator and specified in CALCULATOR_METHODOLOGY.md. Related entry: Planning Trigger.

Evidence posture

Project-defined. Defined within the HygieneRoll model. No external evidence is attached.

HRK-005

HygieneRoll working definition

Illustrative Cadence

Definition

Within HygieneRoll calculator v0.1, illustrative cadence estimates how long a replenishment quantity would cover under the supplied usage assumptions. It is not an optimal subscription frequency, an automatic shipping recommendation, or a guaranteed usage interval.

Key distinction

Cadence here is coverage under a stated quantity and a stated usage rate. If the replenishment quantity is left blank, the calculator uses the current quantity for that illustration. The result is a planning picture, not a schedule the household is expected to keep.

Why it matters inside the model

It shows why a single shipping interval can fit one item and miss another. The label stays “illustrative” so the figure is not read as a recommended subscription.

Related artifact

Specified in CALCULATOR_METHODOLOGY.md and shown on the calculator. Related entry: Replenishment vs Subscription.

Evidence posture

Project-defined. Defined within the HygieneRoll model. No external evidence is attached. A numerical walkthrough, if one is shown elsewhere, is an illustrative model rather than observed usage.

HRK-006

HygieneRoll working definition

Ownership of Return

Definition

Within HygieneRoll, ownership of return describes controlling or directly operating the consumer-facing re-entry surface through which recurring household demand can come back. It does not mean legal ownership of consumers, ownership of their behavior, proven retention, or guaranteed recurring revenue.

Key distinction

Retail can distribute a product and still leave the next purchase outside the operator’s direct relationship. Ownership of return, in this model, is about that re-entry surface: the place a household can come back to when the need approaches again. It is a strategic and commercial concept, not a claim that demand has already been captured.

Why it matters inside the model

The asset is built around the idea that recurring hygiene is valuable when the return path is direct. The reference states the idea. It does not report retention, revenue, or an operating history.

Related artifact

Stated on the homepage and carried through the replenishment model. Related entries: Re-entry and Category Expansion.

Evidence posture

Project-defined. Defined within the HygieneRoll model. No external evidence is attached.

HRK-007

HygieneRoll working definition

Re-entry

Definition

Within the HygieneRoll model, re-entry is the point at which a household returns to a consumer surface because a recurring need is approaching again. It is distinct from a first purchase.

Key distinction

A first purchase opens the relationship. Re-entry is the later interaction the model is built to represent: need, use, replenishment, and return. The model does not claim that every household behaves this way. It names the event the loop is organized around.

Why it matters inside the model

Without a distinct return event, replenishment collapses into a storefront that waits for the next unrelated visit. Re-entry keeps the next cycle attached to the same household need.

Related artifact

Described on the homepage and in the replenishment model. Related entries: Household Depletion Clock and Ownership of Return.

Project-defined. Defined within the HygieneRoll model. No external evidence is attached.

HRK-008

HygieneRoll working definition

Category Expansion

Definition

The HygieneRoll category model treats paper hygiene as the clearest entry anchor, then personal care, then broader household hygiene. Paper hygiene is not the category ceiling. This sequence is a strategic model, not a claim that it is the empirically best expansion path.

Key distinction

Paper hygiene — tissue, rolls, towels, wipes — is where the household language is already specific. Personal care and household cleaning are the expansion the model is built to hold. The order is an architecture choice. It is not a market-leadership claim and not a finding about the most profitable sequence.

Why it matters inside the model

The same replenishment relationship can cover more than one clock. Expansion, in this reference, means the surface is designed to hold those further essentials, not that a household has been observed to adopt them in this order.

Related artifact

Stated on the homepage and in the replenishment model. Related entry: Ownership of Return.

How evidence enters the reference

An external fact is admitted only when a source exists, the source supports that exact sentence, and the scope, period, geography, population, and metric match. If any of those fail, the claim is blocked. It is not published in weaker wording that still asserts the fact.

Project pages are provenance for definitions. They are not evidence about the outside world. Interpretation, when it eventually appears, will name its sources and stay in interpretive language. Illustrative scenarios will stay labeled as illustrations.

The source register for v0.1 is empty on purpose. Authority in this foundation is the refusal to blur a definition into an observation.

Apply the model

The calculator turns these definitions into a planning window from the visitor’s own inputs. The replenishment model is the operating reference the calculator implements. The homepage states the asset thesis those tools belong to.
